agreement).Diabetes is one of the most common chronic diseases in the UK, and the prevalence is
increasing [NICE, 2019b (/topics/diabetes-type-1/references/)].
Diabetes UK estimates that in the UK [Diabetes UK, 2019 (/topics/diabetes-type-
1/references/)]:
About 4.7 million people have diabetes. 
About 36,000 children under 19 years of age have diabetes (most children are
diagnosed between the ages of 10 and 14 years).
More than 5 million people will be diagnosed with diabetes by 2025, and more than
5.5 million by 2030.
Type 1 diabetes most commonly presents in children and young people, persists into
and can start in adult life [NICE, 2015a (/topics/diabetes-type-1/references/)].
It can present at any age, although incidence peaks in early childhood (age 6 months
to 5 years) and again during puberty[NICE, 2015a (/topics/diabetes-type-
1/references/)].
About 8% of people with diabetes have type 1 diabetes [Diabetes UK, 2019
(/topics/diabetes-type-1/references/)], and the prevalence is highest in people aged
35–60 years of age [NICE, 2015a (/topics/diabetes-type-1/references/)].
About 90% of children and young people in the UK who have diabetes will have type
1 diabetes [Diabetes UK, 2019 (/topics/diabetes-type-1/references/)].
