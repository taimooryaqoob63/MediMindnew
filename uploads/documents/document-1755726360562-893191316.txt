Diabetes - type 2:
How commo n is it?
Last revised in March 2025
Diabetes is one of the most common chronic diseases in the UK, and its prevalence is
increasing [NICE, 2020a (/topics/diabetes-type-2/references/)]. 
In 2018–2019 there were 3,919,505 people diagnosed with diabetes [Diabetes UK,
2019b (/topics/diabetes-type-2/references/)].
About 90% of adults currently diagnosed have type 2 diabetes [Diabetes UK, 2019a
(/topics/diabetes-type-2/references/)].
About one million people with type 2 diabetes are currently undiagnosed [Diabetes
UK, 2019a (/topics/diabetes-type-2/references/)].
A primary care cohort study analysis of the General Practice Research Database of
1.3 million patients in England and Wales estimated [Ryan, 2005 (/topics/diabetes-
type-2/references/)]:
The rate of diagnosis of new cases of diabetes (type not specified) was 17.5 per
10,000 person-years in 1994 and 22.1 per 10,000 person-years in 1998,
representing an increase in new cases of 26% over the study period.
The National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) estimates that by 2025,
more than 5 million people in the UK will be diagnosed with diabetes [NICE, 2020a
(/topics/diabetes-type-2/references/)].
Type 2 diabetes can occur in all age groups and is increasingly being diagnosed in
children[NICE, 2020a (/topics/diabetes-type-2/references/)].
About 36,000 children and young people under the age of 19 years have diabetes, of
which about 10% have type 2 diabetes (or other rarer types of diabetes) [Diabetes
UK, 2019a (/topics/diabetes-type-2/references/)].
A UK and Republic of Ireland prospective monthly surveillance study reported a total
of 106 new cases of type 2 diabetes diagnosed in children and young people aged
less than 17 years between 1st April 2015 and 30th April 2016, equating to a UK
incidence of 0.72 per 100,000 children and young people per year [Candler, 2018b
(/topics/diabetes-type-2/references/)].
Children from Asian and Black and Afro-Caribbean ethnic groups had a
significantly higher incidence than white children, as did girls compared with boys.
There was a trend in increased incidence from 2005 to 2015.
It concludes that type 2 diabetes remains much less common than type 1 diabetes
in childhood in the UK, but the number of cases diagnosed is rising.
The trends in reported global incidence of type 2 diabetes vary in the literature
depending on the country, age group, and ethnicity of the population studied.
A Global Burden of Disease Study analysis found [Liu, 2020 (/topics/diabetes-type-
2/references/)]:
The global age-standardized incidence rate of type 2 diabetes significantly
increased from 228 per 100,000 people in 1990 to 279 per 100,000 people in 2017,
with an estimated annual percentage change of 0.89.
A systematic review of 42 global population-based studies of diabetes incidence
trends found the incidence of total or type 2 diabetes in the populations studied
[Magliano, 2019 (/topics/diabetes-type-2/references/)]:
Increased in 66%, stabilized in 32%, and decreased in 2% from 1990 to 2005.
