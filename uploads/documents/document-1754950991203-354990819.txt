[SECTION-INSULIN] Insulin deficiency in type 2 diabetes progresses with time and usually worsens over a
period of years [NICE, 2020a (/topics/diabetes-type-2/references/)].
The International Society for Pediatric and Adolescent Diabetes (ISPAD) guidelines
note that type 2 diabetes diagnosed in young people is characterized by rapid onset
and progression, with more common and progressive co-morbidities (such as fatty
liver disease and sleep apnoea), and more rapid development of microvascular
complications and cardiovascular risk than adult-onset type 2 diabetes, leading to
increased morbidity and mortality rates [Zeitler, 2018 (/topics/diabetes-type-
2/references/)].
The World Health Organization notes that young-onset type 2 diabetes is associated
with greater mortality rates, more complications, and increased cardiovascular
disease risk factors than for people with type 1 diabetes of similar duration [WHO,
2019 (/topics/diabetes-type-2/references/)].
