Diabetes - type 1:
What are the causes and risk factors?

agreement).Type 1 diabetes is caused by an absolute insulin deficiency, usually resulting from
autoimmune destruction of the insulin-producing beta cells in the pancreas.
Risk factors include:
Genetic factors — type 1 diabetes is a heritable polygenic disease.
A sibling of a person with type 1 diabetes has 6–7% risk of developing it
themselves. This risk increases to 30–70% in identical twins.
The risk for a child is 1–9% if a parent has type 1 diabetes.
Several different alleles (alternative forms of a gene) and haplotypes
(combinations of alleles) have been associated with either an increased or
decreased risk of type 1 diabetes. However, the mechanisms by which these
haplotypes interact and alter risk are not completely understood. 
Environmental factors — there is evidence that in genetically susceptible people,
certain environmental factors can trigger the development of autoimmunity to the
pancreatic beta cells. 
Environmental factors associated with type 1 diabetes include diet, vitamin D
exposure, obesity, early-life exposure to viruses associated with islet inflammation
(such as enteroviruses), and decreased gut-microbiome diversity.
The importance of environmental risk factors (regardless of genetic background
risk) are highlighted by the large differences in the incidence of type 1 diabetes in
genetically similar populations that are separated by socioeconomic borders and
by the increasing incidence of type 1 diabetes in genetically low-risk people.
